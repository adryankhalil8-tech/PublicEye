## Summary

Two sets of naval oil leases, one Interior Secretary, and very different
legal outcomes:

- **Elk Hills (California) → Edward L. Doheny.** On November 30, 1921, Doheny
  sent Fall $100,000 in cash, carried to Washington by Doheny's son. The
  leases were later cancelled by the Supreme Court as "consummated by
  conspiracy, corruption and fraud" (1927, civil).
- **Teapot Dome (Wyoming) → Harry F. Sinclair.** Fall signed the lease to
  Sinclair's Mammoth Oil Company on April 7, 1922, in secret. The trial court
  found **no fraud**; the appeals court and Supreme Court found fraud and
  conspiracy (1927, civil) — but the Supreme Court expressly **did not decide
  whether Fall was bribed** over this lease. $200,000 of Liberty Bonds traced
  by serial number to a shell company reached Fall's son-in-law; Sinclair gave
  Fall $25,000 after he left office.

**Criminal outcomes (the spine of the story):**

## Traps

- **Civil vs. criminal.** "Fraud", "conspiracy", and "corruption" in the 1927
  Supreme Court opinions are civil findings cancelling leases. Sinclair and
  Doheny were **acquitted** criminally. Never write "Sinclair conspired" or
  "Doheny bribed Fall" as fact.
- **The bribery verdicts point in opposite directions.** Fall was convicted
  of _accepting_ a bribe from Doheny; Doheny was acquitted of _giving_ it.
  Both are true. The courts explained why both can stand (the intent of each
  man was separately at issue).
- **No bribery finding on Teapot Dome.** The Supreme Court declined to decide
  whether Fall was bribed over the Sinclair lease.
- **William J. Burns** was convicted of contempt and then **reversed**.
- **Taking the Fifth is not guilt.** Fall's son-in-law invoked the privilege.
- **Bounded facts.** "Fall has never gone to jail" is true as of March 1930
  only.
- **Unverified popular claims** (first cabinet officer imprisoned; Fall's
  prison term) are recorded as UNVERIFIED and must not be used yet.
- **Quotes in context.** Counsel's "deliberately lied" sentence continues
  "…as he had a right to do". Don't truncate it to change its meaning.
